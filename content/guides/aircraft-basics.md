You do not need to memorize an airplane to begin learning from one. Start by looking for the systems and surfaces that make every flight possible.

### The outside view

- **Wings** create lift and usually carry key components such as fuel tanks, flaps, and ailerons.
- **The tail** provides stability; the elevator and rudder help control pitch and yaw.
- **Landing gear** supports the airplane on the ground and changes how it is handled during taxi, takeoff, and landing.
- **Control surfaces** move airflow, not the airplane directly. That is why airspeed, trim, and smooth inputs matter.

### The inside view

In a training airplane, look for the primary flight instruments, engine instruments, fuel information, electrical controls, and the checklist. Their exact layout varies by airplane. A familiar-looking panel is never a reason to skip the actual aircraft's checklist or pilot's operating handbook.

### A good first question

When you see an airplane, ask: *What does this part do, and how would a pilot know if it is working normally?* That question connects design, preflight inspection, and decision-making.

The FAA's [Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/phak) has chapters on aircraft construction, flight controls, systems, instruments, and performance. Read it alongside a real airplane only with appropriate supervision.
