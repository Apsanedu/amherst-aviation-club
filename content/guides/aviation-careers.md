Piloting is only one part of aviation. Airports, airlines, cargo operations, manufacturers, public agencies, museums, and research organizations rely on people with many different skills.

### Career areas to explore

- **Flight operations:** pilots, dispatchers, flight attendants, and operations coordinators.
- **Technical work:** aircraft maintenance technicians, avionics technicians, engineers, and manufacturing specialists.
- **Airspace and airports:** air traffic controllers, airport operations staff, safety specialists, and planners.
- **Aerospace and research:** designers, analysts, software specialists, technicians, and project managers.

### Investigate a role, not just a title

For a job that interests you, find out what training is required, where people usually begin, what the schedule is like, and which credentials are legally required. Distinguish between an employer's current hiring program and a long-term guarantee: pathways, costs, and eligibility can change.

The [Tuskegee Airmen Scholarship Foundation's career overview](https://www.taisf.org/launch-a-career-in-aviation.html) is a useful broad survey. For airline and cargo pathways, see the club's [Career & Scholarships resources]({{< relref "/resources" >}}#career-scholarships), then confirm every detail with the organization offering it.
