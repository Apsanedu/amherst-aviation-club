An introductory or discovery lesson is a chance to see whether flying appeals to you. It is also a chance to learn how an instructor teaches and how a school approaches safety, scheduling, and costs.

### Before booking

- Ask what the lesson includes: time in the airplane, ground briefing, instructor time, and any fees.
- Ask what kind of airplane you will fly and what happens if weather requires a cancellation.
- Tell the instructor what you hope to learn. It is fine if the answer is simply, “I want to see what flying feels like.”

### During the visit

Notice whether explanations are clear, questions are welcome, and the operation feels organized. You do not need to decide on a complete training package after one visit. If you want to continue, compare more than one instructor or school and ask for a realistic discussion of availability, total costs, and the pace that suits you.

### Keep expectations practical

No club, website, or simulator can tell you which training route is right for you. A CFI can explain the next appropriate steps for your goals, including when paperwork or medical certification becomes relevant.

The FAA's [Become a Pilot](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/become) guide is the right official starting point for certificate options; the club's [Resources]({{< relref "/resources" >}}) page collects study and career links to explore before you commit.
