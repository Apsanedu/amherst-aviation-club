A simulator is excellent for building curiosity and repetition. It can help you understand what controls do, recognize an airport layout, and practice a simple flow without paying for aircraft time. It is not a substitute for instruction, real-world weather judgment, or aircraft handling with a CFI.

### Four useful simulator sessions

1. **Learn the cockpit.** Pick one simple training airplane and identify the primary flight controls, engine instruments, and basic radios.
2. **Fly the traffic pattern.** Use a familiar airport, pause often, and learn the sequence of departure, crosswind, downwind, base, and final.
3. **Practice navigation awareness.** Plan a short route, identify checkpoints, and notice how heading, wind, and groundspeed differ.
4. **Review a real lesson.** If you are training, ask your instructor which concept is appropriate to rehearse. Do not invent procedures from a game setting.

### Make practice deliberate

Set one goal per session: for example, hold a heading, understand a checklist flow, or identify airport markings. End by writing down one question for a CFI or for your next ground-school session.

The FAA notes that flight simulation supports training and safety work, but qualified training devices and ordinary home simulators are not interchangeable. For a free place to explore, see [FlightGear](https://www.flightgear.org/); for formal credit or training decisions, ask a CFI what applies to your situation.
