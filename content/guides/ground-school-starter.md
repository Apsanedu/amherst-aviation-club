Ground school is the knowledge side of learning to fly. It is not separate from the airplane: weather affects a planned flight, airspace affects where it can go, and aircraft systems affect what a pilot checks before starting.

### Start with these five subjects

1. **How airplanes fly** — lift, drag, stability, stalls, and flight controls.
2. **Aircraft systems** — engine, fuel, electrical, instruments, and the pilot’s operating handbook for the specific airplane.
3. **Weather** — observation, forecasts, fronts, wind, visibility, and the limits of a forecast.
4. **Airspace and airport operations** — how pilots share the national airspace system and communicate safely.
5. **Navigation and decision-making** — charts, route planning, risk management, and knowing when not to go.

Read with a question in mind rather than trying to memorize a whole handbook at once. For example: after reading about weather, look at a real airport weather report with a CFI or experienced pilot and ask what it means in context.

### Official FAA reading

The FAA’s [Pilot’s Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/phak) covers the core subjects above. The FAA’s [handbooks and manuals library](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals) is the best starting point for current editions of the Airplane Flying Handbook and related references.

Use a CFI or an approved course to connect study material to real training and to confirm what applies to your certificate goal.
