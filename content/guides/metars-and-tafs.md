Every flight starts with two coded weather products for the departure and destination airports. They look like gibberish for about a week, then become second nature.

### The two reports

1. **METAR** — what the weather is doing *right now*: wind, visibility, clouds, temperature, pressure. Updated hourly.
2. **TAF** — what is *forecast* for the next 24–30 hours, in 6-hour chunks. This is the go/no-go document.

### Decode in this order

Wind first (can you handle the crosswind?), then visibility and ceiling (is it VFR?), then trends in the TAF (is it getting better or worse while you fly?).

### Build the habit

Read this guide's companion, [Aviation Weather: A Beginner's Reading Routine](/guides/aviation-weather-starter/), then pull a real METAR for the nearest field before every club meeting and brief it in one sentence: *"VFR, windy, getting worse after 3 PM."*
