A sectional chart is the visual-flight pilot's road map. It looks busy at first, but you only need four things on a first read: airports, airspace, terrain, and your route between checkpoints.

### Start with these four features

1. **Airports** — blue circles have control towers; magenta circles do not. Tick marks around the circle mean fuel is available.
2. **Airspace** — solid blue and magenta lines mark controlled airspace boundaries. Read the numbers inside the boxes as altitude limits before worrying about the rest.
3. **Terrain** — bold numbers like **38** are maximum elevation figures for that square. Brown shading means higher ground nearby.
4. **Checkpoints** — towns, lakes, roads, and towers you can actually see from the cockpit. Pick features that are hard to confuse with each other.

### Try it on the club chart

Find the nearest airport to campus on a current [FAA sectional](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/digital_products/vfr/), trace a short route to a neighboring field, and list the airspace you would cross. Bring it to club night and compare answers.

Charts expire on a 56-day cycle, so always check the effective date in the margin before planning anything real.
